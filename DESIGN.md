# Zombie Survival FPS design

This file has the core loop and the decided game rules: the decisions that the code does not show. [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) has the commands and the code rules.

Edit this file only when the core loop or a decided rule changes. Do not list the parts of the game or the tunable values here. Each feature folder and its tests in `tests/<folder>/` give the exact behavior. The `@export` values and `.tres` Resources hold the tunable values, and the Inspector shows them. The verify skill's feature files in [.claude/skills/verify/features/](.claude/skills/verify/features/) show what a player can do with each part, and how to prove it. GitHub issues hold the work that is not done.

## The core loop

You stand in an arena. Zombies come at you in rounds, and you shoot them before they reach you. When all zombies of a round are dead, a short break starts. Then a harder round starts. When your health gets to 0, the game ends and shows the round that you reached. With a self-revive perk you are downed first: you are low, you move slowly, and you can fire only the pistol. The perk stands you up after a short time. If the downed timer ends first, the game ends. You restart and try to reach a later round.

## Decided rules

- The zombie is the cartoon Shambler model, `zombie/zombie_model.glb`, in the same style as the pistol: beveled primitives, flat colors, and an ink outline. Its source is `art_source/zombie.blend`. Edit the `.blend` in Blender (through the Blender MCP or by hand), then export the `.glb` from it. Do not add a build script. The model has six parts with no rig: `LegL`, `LegR`, and `Torso`, with `Head`, `ArmL`, and `ArmR` under `Torso`. Each part has its origin on its joint. Keep these names and origins, and `ZombieAnimator` (`zombie/zombie_animator.gd`) animates them in code, as `view_model.gd` animates the pistol. A PS1-style generated model with rigged animation was tried and rejected.
- The player's body is the cartoon Scout model, `player/player_model.glb`, in the same style. Its source is `art_source/player.blend`, edited and exported like the zombie. Its parts are `LegL` and `LegR`, each with a shin (`ShinL`, `ShinR`) that has a foot (`FootL`, `FootR`), and `Torso`, with `Head`, `ArmL`, and `ArmR` under `Torso`. Each part has its origin on its joint, and L and R are the model's own left and right. `PlayerAnimator` (`player/player_animator.gd`) poses them in code: a walk that follows the speed, a stride in the air and on a slide, bent knees with flat feet while crouched, a right arm that holds the gun where the player looks, and both arms up while aiming. In first person the body draws only its shadow, so the player sees their own shadow. In a debug build, **F5** (the `third_person` action) puts the camera behind the player and shows the body.
- In first person, the Scout's right hand holds the pistol. It is `player/player_hand.glb`, from the `ViewArm` scene in `art_source/player.blend`. A `RemoteTransform3D` on the gun moves it, so it kicks with the gun. During a reload it follows the thrown gun without the spin, and comes back with the new gun. The SMG (`MagazineViewModel`) keeps the gun in the hand instead: its reload tilts the gun, drops the empty magazine as debris, and the left fist pushes a new one up into the well. The gun and hand neither cast nor receive shadows, because they float in front of the camera.
- The level uses Godot primitives and code for meshes and materials. The only imported models are the weapon view models, `weapons/pistol.glb` and `weapons/smg.glb` (Blender sources `art_source/pistol.blend` and `art_source/smg.blend`), the zombie, and the player.
- Weapons are hitscan. A shot is one ray from the camera.
- A shot that hits a `WeakSpot` (`health/weak_spot.gd`) multiplies its damage by `WeakSpot.damage_multiplier`, and the HUD shows the hit marker in the headshot color. The zombie's head is a `WeakSpot` that follows the animated head, and a dead zombie's head is off. Punches do not headshot.
- The downed state belongs to `Player`. Solo, the player dies at once at 0 health. Only a held perk with a `PerkItem.self_revive_time` above 0 (SECOND WIND) downs the player instead. The perk stands the player up after its time. If `Player.downed_time` ends first, the player dies (`died` and the game over screen follow). A downed player has the pistol with a full magazine, does not sprint, jump, melee, switch weapons, or interact, and zombies still attack. `Player.revive(fraction)` is the API that a perk calls to stand the player up.
- The player cannot shoot while sprinting.
- The slide is the fast way to move. There is no bunny hop: each jump needs a new press of jump, and air control never takes the speed above the speed at takeoff or the target speed.
- A node that can take damage has a `Health` child (`health/health.gd`). Code that deals damage finds it with `Health.find_on(node)`.
- `RoundSettings` (`rounds/round_settings.tres`) is the only source of zombie count, health, and speed for each round.
- Points come from shots and punches that hit a living zombie (`Wallet.points_per_hit`) and from the killing blow (`Wallet.kill_bonus`). A headshot kill adds `Wallet.headshot_bonus`. Target dummies and corpses give none. The `Wallet` on the player is the only owner of the balance.
- The player holds at most two weapons (`Player.max_weapons`) and switches with **1**, **2**, and the mouse wheel. Buying a new weapon while two are held replaces the weapon in hand. A holstered weapon does not fire, and its reload stops with no refill.
- A weapon station is a `BuyStation` with a `WeaponItem`. It sells the weapon, and once the player holds it, that weapon's ammo.
- The reserve is per weapon (`WeaponData.max_reserve`). A reload moves rounds from the reserve into the magazine, and with an empty reserve there is no reload.
- A `BuyStation` sells one `BuyItem` resource (`economy/`). A subclass of `BuyItem` says when the item can be bought and what it gives (`AmmoItem` fills the reserve). A new thing to buy, such as a weapon or a door, is a new `BuyItem` subclass and a `.tres`, not a new station.
- A door is a `BuyStation` that sells a `DoorItem` (`doors/`). Buying it opens the door for good: its station has no collider, and the level bakes the navigation mesh again, so zombies path through the gap.
- A `SpawnPoint` (`rounds/`) lists the doors of its area. It spawns zombies only while one of its doors is open. A point that lists no door always spawns. `RoundManager` spawns only at `SpawnPoint` children.
- A pickup (`pickups/`) gives a `BuyItem` to the player for free when the item would do something. Otherwise it stays on the ground. A zombie drops one by chance when it dies, by its `DropTable`. Double points multiplies only earned points (hits, kills, boards) through `Wallet.boost`, and the board limit counts the points before the boost.
- A barricade (`barricades/`) is a window or a gate with boards. A zombie that comes through it stops, and pulls off one board at a time, and it walks through when none are left. The player repairs only within `Barricade.repair_range` of the opening, and a zombie at a barricade with boards hits a player in that same range and pulls no board while it hits. So a repair always costs health. Each zombie at the barricade pulls boards on its own timer. The player holds the interact action to put boards back, and each board gives points through the `Wallet`, up to a limit in each round. A `SpawnPoint` names the barricade that its zombies come through, and they walk to it before they chase the player. The barricade body is on physics layer 3, so only the player collides with it. Zombies and shots pass its plane.
- A `PowerSwitch` (`perks/`) turns on once with the interact action and stays on. A `BuyStation` that names a switch in `power` sells only while that switch is on. Each level has one switch, behind a door, and every perk machine names it.
- A perk machine is a `BuyStation` that sells a `PerkItem`. A perk changes a stat of the player while it is held: max health (the bonus is also added to the current health), the reload time, the time between shots, or a self-revive. `Perks` on the player is the only owner of the held perks. The player holds one of each perk, at most `Perks.max_held`, and loses all of them at 0 health, so a self-revive is used up when it downs the player. A perk can cost more each time the player takes it in a run (`PerkItem.price_increase`) and can sell only `PerkItem.max_buys` times in a run. Then its station shows SOLD OUT. `Perks` counts the buys, and the restart resets the count. SECOND WIND costs 1500, 3000, then 4500, and sells 3 times. The HUD shows an icon for each held perk.
- An upgrade machine is a `BuyStation` that sells an `UpgradeItem` (`upgrades/`) and names the power switch. It swaps the weapon in hand for its `WeaponData.upgrade`, in the same slot, with a full magazine and reserve. An upgraded weapon has no upgrade, so each weapon is upgraded one time. The upgraded data swaps named materials of the gun model (`WeaponData.view_materials`), so it looks different and keeps its ink outline. A weapon station counts an upgraded weapon as its own and sells its ammo.
